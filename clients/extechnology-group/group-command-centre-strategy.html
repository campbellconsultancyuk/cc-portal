SECTION 01

The problem

Each business in the group runs well on its own systems. The problem is not the software. The problem is that the group's only integration layer is Cian.

Sales enquiries, quotations, invoices, management accounts, cash positions and operational KPIs each live in the right system for the job: OnePageCRM and Xero at Extechnology, Loc8 and Xero at Arden, Sage Business Cloud and spreadsheets at J Younger, with Microsoft 365 and banking across all three. Every one of those platforms does its own job reasonably well.

What none of them does is talk to the others. Information moves between systems by hand, reports are compiled by hand, and the live picture of the group exists in one place only: assembled each month, from memory and spreadsheets, by the Group Director. That has three consequences:

  • Time is spent joining, not deciding. Hours each month go into pulling data together before any decision can be made on it. The current spend on admin and operational support sits at £1,500 to £3,000 per month before that time cost is counted.
  • The picture is always historical. By the time the group view is assembled it describes last month, not this morning. Cash, pipeline and job status questions get answered by logging into three systems, not one.
  • Growth makes it worse, mechanically. Every additional company, employee or process adds to the joining work. On the current model, acquiring a fourth business means acquiring its admin as well. That is the exact trend the group wants reversed.
The design brief, in one line

Not "automate what the group does today", but "design the operating framework the group would build from scratch, knowing where it wants to be in five years". This document is a response to that brief.

SECTION 02

The solution

Build the framework once. Every company, present or future, plugs into the same three-layer structure: automated pipelines, one central database, one command centre on top.

The approach keeps every system the group has already invested in. Nothing is replaced. Instead, an automation layer catches events from each platform as they happen, translates them into one consistent format, tags every record with the company it belongs to, and writes it to a single central database. The Command Centre reads from that database in real time. One URL, opened each morning, showing the whole group: live revenue, cash balances, debtors, pipeline and operational KPIs, per company and in aggregate.

Deliberately phased around what is proven today

Platform research across the group is complete, and it splits the build cleanly:

  • Extechnology Europe and Arden Environmental go first. Every core platform at these two companies has a confirmed, workable integration route today. Phase 1 builds their pipelines and stands up the Command Centre with two live companies in it.
  • J Younger Storage is deliberately held back, and that is a feature. Two genuine open questions remain there: stock records have historically not been kept electronically, and the volume-based billing model means stock data is the foundation of any meaningful KPI for that business. Rather than guess, J Younger becomes the live demonstration of the architecture's core claim: that a new company drops into the existing framework without rebuilding anything. Section 03 shows exactly how.
Why this sequencing matters

Most integration projects fail by attempting everything at once and stalling on the hardest data source. This plan proves the framework on the two companies that are ready, then onboards the third using the same repeatable playbook that would onboard an acquisition. J Younger is not deferred. It is the first test of the thing the group actually wants to buy: scalability.

What "done" looks like

  • One live dashboard covering the group, updating in near real time, no manual compilation.
  • A nightly AI-written digest summarising each company's day: cash movement, invoices raised and paid, jobs completed, pipeline changes.
  • Routine information movement between systems happening automatically, with the human touch kept where it belongs: customer relationships, judgement calls and exceptions.
  • A documented, repeatable onboarding playbook, proven on J Younger, ready for company four.

Company Zero: the five-year test

The brief was to design as if starting from scratch, knowing where the group wants to be in five years. The honest test of that is not the first three companies. It is company four, the one that does not exist yet. The architecture is designed so that onboarding any new company, whether J Younger this year or an acquisition in year three, is the same four-step routine:

StepActionWhat changes
1Add one row to the companies tableDatabase schema: unchanged
2Duplicate the relevant pipeline templatesAutomation layer: copies, not rebuilds
3Point the copies at the new company's credentialsSource systems: untouched
4The Command Centre picks the company up automaticallyFrontend: zero changes
J Younger is the proof, not the exception

J Younger Storage will be the first company onboarded through this exact routine, once its Phase 0 stock-record work completes. The group gets to watch its own "acquisition drill" run end to end before ever needing it for a real acquisition. If onboarding J Younger takes more than the four steps above, the architecture has failed its own test, and that is the standard Campbell Consultancy is asking to be held to.

SECTION 04

Beyond the dashboard

The Command Centre is the foundation, not the ceiling. Once the group's data flows through one structured database, three further capabilities open up at low marginal cost.

4.1  Operations over WhatsApp

A dashboard answers questions when someone opens it. Most operational questions do not arrive at a desk. They arrive on site, in a van, or at 7am before the day starts.

Campbell Consultancy runs a live internal WhatsApp assistant for Fan Rescue, an existing client. Their team queries operational data and triggers routine workflows from the same chat thread they already use all day, with no new app, no login and no training beyond "message it like a colleague". It is an internal tool rather than a customer-facing one, and that is precisely the shape that fits this group.

Applied here: the Command Centre's data becomes queryable by message. "Cash position across the group?" "Which Arden jobs are overdue?" "Did the Hendon invoice get paid?" Each answered in seconds, from the same live database the dashboard reads, with the nightly digest delivered to WhatsApp rather than waiting to be opened.

Why this is mentioned now

Not as a Phase 1 deliverable, but because it changes nothing about the architecture. The database and automation layer being proposed already support it. It is a bolt-on decision for later, not a design decision for now.

4.2  AI agents: from reporting to acting

The nightly digest described in Section 03 is AI in its most conservative role: reading structured data and writing a summary. Agents are the next step on the same foundation: software that can carry out a bounded task end to end, with rules about what it may do alone and what needs a human decision.

Internal-facing agents

  • Credit control assistant. Watches the invoice table, flags overdue accounts, drafts chase correspondence for approval, escalates patterns worth a phone call.
  • Anomaly watch. Flags the unusual before it is asked about: a cash dip, a stalled pipeline stage, a job overrunning its schedule.
  • Report preparation. Assembles the monthly management pack from live data, in the group's own format, for review rather than compilation.

External-facing agents · later phase

  • Enquiry triage. First response to inbound sales enquiries, gathering the details a human needs before quoting, then routing to the right person.
  • Quote follow-up. Polite, well-timed follow-ups on outstanding quotations, handing over the moment a reply arrives.
  • Booking and availability. For the storage business, handling routine availability and booking queries once its data foundation exists.

The sequencing rule is deliberate: internal agents first, where mistakes are visible and cheap; external agents only once the internal ones have earned trust, and always with the human touch preserved where the group has said it matters. Agents act on structured data, which is exactly what Phases 1 and 2 create. This is the compounding return on the same foundation, not a separate project.

4.3  Digital presence

Alongside the operational work, Campbell Consultancy has experience across the group's wider digital footprint: search visibility, website user experience, content management platforms, lead generation and live AI chat on the web including WhatsApp. Initial analysis of the group's websites has already been carried out. When the time is right, Campbell Consultancy can recommend the right path to increase online visibility and, through it, client acquisition. That conversation is separate from this build and is flagged here only so the group knows the capability exists under the same roof.

SECTION 05

Value & return

The return is measured in three currencies: recovered time at director level, decisions made on live data rather than last month's, and growth that no longer drags its own admin behind it.

The hidden line
Director hours
the most expensive hours in the group, currently spent compiling reports and chasing information rather than on sales, strategy and acquisitions.
The scaling effect
Company four
on the current model, each new company adds admin. On this framework, it adds a database row and copied templates. The gap between those two compounds with every acquisition.

An honest framing

No percentage savings figure is quoted in this document, deliberately. Numbers of that kind are only worth the data behind them, and the honest baseline measurement of hours currently spent on manual reporting and data movement is itself one of the first things the build will surface. What can be said plainly:

  • The recurring cost of the platform is modest. The underlying infrastructure runs on proven, low-cost tools, and the running cost is wrapped into the ongoing engagement rather than arriving as a stack of separate software bills.
  • The savings are structural, not one-off. Manual reporting does not get faster; it gets removed. Every workflow automated in Phase 1 stays automated for every future company at near-zero marginal cost.
  • The real return is capacity. The group's stated ambition is acquisition-led growth on a systemised playbook. The binding constraint on that ambition today is the Group Director's time. That is the asset this framework is designed to release.

Proposed next step

If the shape of this strategy is right, the next step is a working session to agree Phase 1 scope for Extechnology Europe and Arden Environmental, confirm the Loc8 OData route, and define the Phase 0 discovery piece for J Younger's stock records. Commercial structure and pricing follow from that agreed scope, not the other way round.

The standard this work is held to

Adding a company to the group should be an administrative non-event. Everything in this document is in service of that one sentence.
